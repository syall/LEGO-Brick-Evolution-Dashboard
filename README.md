One of my favorite topics is the evolution of the Lego Brick, both with variety, specialization, and color palette. I've written a presentation about this topic in the past, but seeing what Perplexity AI could research and synthesize was especially impressive.

This was my initial thread (https://www.perplexity.ai/search/755516dd-46c7-4830-a0da-5d0ad732b246):

> Create a report of the evolution of Lego bricks, spanning in both colors as well as molds.
> - Mark this with timelines, which years had peak specialization, and which years had more consolidation.
> - Compare this with today's products where Lego sets use pieces in creative ways (particularly the Lego Sets for Adults line and my favorite set Lego Tiny Plants #10329).
> - State any multi-year trends across products, and also say what worked financially, creatively, and what is still working.

From there, one suggestions stuck out to me and was spun out into its own thread (https://www.perplexity.ai/computer/tasks/5f97e60c-0ff8-4098-a8f8-28033f1a693d):

> Build an interactive Lego brick evolution dashboard that maps the total number of unique part molds and available colors from 1949 to 2026. Use historical data to plot timelines of 'peak specialization' (years with high new-part introduction) versus 'consolidation' (years where part counts decreased or stagnated). Include an interactive timeline slider that highlights major material shifts like the 1963 switch to ABS plastic and the 2018 introduction of bio-polyethylene elements, and visualize the growth of the part catalog alongside the increase in set complexity for 18+ adult themes

The dashboard that is hosted here is the generated interactive Lego Brick Evolution Dashboard which is extremely impressive visually, to the point where I felt compelled to share this.
